\subsection{Suspension and resumption}
\label{sec:obs-suspend}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=term]
23:34:30  load 11.12                                Busy
23:34:40  SUSPEND is TRUE     Busy -> Suspended         (threshold 12.0)
23:36:45  CONTINUE is TRUE    Suspended -> Busy         (resumed at 7.95)
\end{lstlisting}

The two-minute gap between suspension and resumption is the hysteresis working:
the machine had to fall from 12.0 to below 8.0 before jobs resumed, rather than
oscillating around a single threshold.

00:19:09  job re-matched, evicted again while pressure persisted
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Admission closure under saturation}
\label{sec:closure}

The clause \kn{TotalLoadAvg < TotalCpus * 0.90} is the one easiest to dismiss as
redundant. It is not.

Measured with ten jobs on the sixteen-core machine, each of which
\emph{requested} one core and \emph{used} two:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=term]
free=5   total=19.20   nonCondor=1.26   START=false
\end{lstlisting}

Read the three numbers together:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Condor's \textbf{accounting} says five cores are free and it should send
        more work.
  \item \textbf{Reality} is 120\% oversubscribed.
  \item The \textbf{owner} is idle, so \kn{NonCondorLoad} of 1.26 sits far below
        its 8.0 threshold and that clause is fully permissive.
\end{itemize}

\begin{keypoint}
Only the saturation ceiling closes admission here. Without it the machine would
advertise free capacity at load 19.2 and keep accepting --- owner protected,
machine drowning. Slot accounting is request-based and structurally cannot see
this. Only \file{/proc} can.
\end{keypoint}

\subsubsection{An artifact worth knowing}

When a burst of jobs exits, \kn{CondorLoadAvg} drops immediately while
\kn{TotalLoadAvg} decays over about a minute. Their load therefore briefly
reattributes to non-Condor and can close admission for roughly 30 seconds
afterwards.

This is self-correcting, and it explains a \kn{START=false} that appears just as
a machine goes quiet --- which otherwise looks like a policy bug at exactly the
moment you would least expect one.

\subsection{The user PC, end to end}


The acceptance test for the fourth machine, and the first exercise of the
symmetric case: eight jobs submitted \emph{from} the machine that also executes
them.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=term]
25 0  slot1_1@entry-node        25 4  slot1_2@entry-node
25 1  slot1_1@in-use-desktop    25 5  slot1_2@flagship
25 2  slot1_1@user-pc           25 6  slot1_2@user-pc
25 3  slot1_1@flagship          25 7  slot1_2@in-use-desktop
\end{lstlisting}

Two per machine across all four, all completed, all output returned, zero bytes
of stderr. The job that ran on the user PC reported:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=term]
ran on : user-pc
as     : nobody       %% no worker accounts, scratch sandbox
nice   : 10           %% JOB_RENICE_INCREMENT applied
cpus   : 1            %% OMP_NUM_THREADS, not enforcement
\end{lstlisting}

Three separate claims are settled by that output. Jobs run unprivileged; the
renice that makes donation safe is actually applied; and \kn{nproc} reports the
request rather than any enforced limit, exactly as \S\ref{sec:instruments}
warns.

The retrieval matters as much as the placement. Output transfer is the direction
that fails when a machine advertises an address its peer cannot reach
(\S\ref{sec:vpn}), so eight sandboxes returning cleanly is the check that the
interface pin was correct --- not the fact that the machine appeared in
\kn{condor\_status}.

\subsection{Test methodology}

Three rules emerged, each from a test that produced a misleading result before
the rule existed.

\textbf{Observe on the machine itself, not through the collector.}
\kn{condor\_status} reads the collector, which is only as fresh as
\kn{UPDATE\_INTERVAL}; it showed a frozen value for two and a half minutes during
one attempt. \kn{condor\_status -direct} is not an alternative on workers,
because it needs the pool signing key to verify the caller's token and only the
entry node has it. Use \file{/proc/loadavg} for the real figure and the startd's
own \file{StartLog} for the policy decision.

\textbf{Build up slowly.} Load was raised in stages rather than jumped, so that a
threshold crossing could be attributed to a known input rather than inferred
afterwards.

\textbf{Bound every generator with \kn{timeout}.} The deadline must be owned by
something that survives the session dying --- a dropped ssh connection or a
closed tmux window must not leave load or an allocation behind on a colleague's
desktop.
