\subsection{Notebooks, and what the pool owes them}
\label{sec:notebooks}

\begin{keypoint}
The pool is a service with one interface, and everything above it is the
client's problem. A submit file, a shell script and a notebook cell calling the
Python bindings arrive at the schedd as identical ClassAds. The pool cannot
tell them apart and does not need to.
\end{keypoint}

An earlier revision listed JupyterHub with \kn{batchspawner} as the next phase,
blocked on shared storage for notebook home directories. That framing conflates
two architectures which have almost nothing in common.

A notebook kernel is \textbf{one process on one machine}. It does not
distribute, and hosting it elsewhere is a placement decision rather than a
parallelism one. On a fleet of four-core mini-PCs that placement is a downgrade,
since a laptop with twelve threads beats any of them.

So the kernel stays on the user's own machine and the \emph{work} fans out. The
kernel is a coordinator, cheap and single-threaded, while parallelism lives in
many short jobs.

\begin{keypoint}
\textbf{Hosted kernels are not a deferred phase here.} JupyterHub with
\kn{batchspawner} would place a long-lived, stateful, interactive process on a
machine whose policy suspends it the moment its owner returns and kills it under
memory pressure. That is not a storage problem to solve later. It is the wrong
shape for this pool.
\end{keypoint}

\subsubsection{Why that is the better fit rather than merely the cheaper one}

It removes by construction every problem that made a hosted kernel awkward. No
shared home directories, because nothing durable lives on a worker. No
long-lived kernel to suspend, so \kn{SUSPEND} never freezes a cell somebody is
watching. No interactive process parked on a colleague's desktop. And the
submitted jobs are short and idempotent, which is what the owner policy of
\S\ref{sec:admission} was written for.

The constraint runs the other way instead. Eviction is routine here, so whatever
a notebook submits must be restartable: a cell fanning out two hundred short
self-contained jobs fits the pool, and a cell submitting one eight-hour job that
holds state does not, however it was submitted. That is a property of the work
rather than of the front end.

\subsection{The next three things}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exercise disk-pressure eviction.} It is the only branch of
        \kn{EVICT\_PRESSURE} never observed firing, and Chapter~\ref{sec:silent}
        is eleven arguments against assuming an unobserved branch works. Fill a
        scratch directory on the flagship worker until the floor is crossed.
        \emph{Deferred by decision, not forgotten.}
  \item \textbf{Give workers the name-resolution guard.} A watchdog now
        restarts a worker that the collector cannot see, which bounds the
        damage, but it acts after roughly ten minutes rather than preventing
        the window.
  \item \textbf{Decide the isolation model.} It is cheap now and expensive later,
        and it gates how far the pool can grow beyond people who already trust
        each other. Now that the pool runs on colleagues' own desktops rather
        than spare hardware, that is no longer a hypothetical question.
\end{enumerate}
